# Spectator Readability Contract

- Twelve visible alternating courses, three identical blocks per course.
- Humpty visibly touches a shallow royal seat; the seat visibly touches the top
  course without hiding it.
- Exactly 24 countable objects per side, organized by silhouette on two edge
  racks rather than heaped on the floor.
- Nine families only: beam, hub, axle, wheel, sheave, drum, plank, rope-and-hook,
  and wedge/foot.
- Four connection classes only: `TENON_LOCK`, `AXLE_BEARING`,
  `KEYED_COAXIAL`, and `ROPE_ATTACH`.
- Beam thickness is at least 10 pixels, an active rope at least 4 pixels, and a
  facing socket at least 8 pixels at the normal camera.
- Port highlights appear only during alignment and disappear after lock.
- Unused pieces stay at the edge; failed pieces remain large and persistent.
- Construction reads as reserve, approach, grasp, carry, stage, align, connect,
  release/support, test, and a visible step clear of the finished assembly.

## Deterministic Reference Fixtures

- Rescue: two long beams and wedge feet form a grounded A-frame around a hub,
  bearing axle, and drum. One existing sheave is mounted on the usable backdrop
  for the fixture, producing a visible drum-to-sheave drive segment and a
  sheave-to-Humpty load segment. The load rope winds in 13 stage units, holds
  for two seconds, and pays out while Humpty remains dynamic.
- Attack: a long square-section beam locks into a hub and bearing axle with two
  keyed wheels. The timber nose contacts a real lower Jenga block; operator
  impulse is distributed across the connected cart, while block motion comes
  only from collision.
- These fixtures are deterministic balance and browser references. They are
  not names, recipes, or strategies exposed to agents.
